# Teach a recruit

Illustrative possible learned output only. Do not preload this authored method or require agents to discover this exact skill.

Version: 0.1.0 authored draft. Learning storage and model teaching are not implemented yet. This is one possible method an experienced actor could develop.

## Goal

The learner can perform a scoped task with fewer interventions on an unseen valid variation. The learner adapts shared knowledge into its own versioned method, with provenance and evidence from its own actions.

## Teach the decisions

Select one discrete outcome the learner is ready to practise. Demonstrate the physical method or explain the decision criteria. Explain why you choose a task, which prerequisites you check, when to ask a colleague, how to verify success and when to stop. Avoid instructions tied only to one position or one named teammate.

Ask the learner to propose their own short draft. Read it for missing prerequisites and unverified assumptions. Save a new immutable draft revision; do not overwrite prior knowledge or edit the learner's tool permissions.

## Let the learner act

During practice, the learner makes their own model decisions and world calls. Observe the outcome. Help if needed, but record the intervention; teacher tool effects cannot be credited to the learner. Discuss the observed failure and revise the draft rather than pretending that a successful explanation proves competence.

## Adapt through shared work

On the next suitable incident, let the learner choose how to apply or change the method. Compare the actual outcome with expectations and discuss a useful correction. Record failures and assistance, preserve previous versions, and branch the method when conditions require a different approach. No examination is required before trying it.

## Evidence

Pass: valid task outcome in practice and transfer, within the scoped intervention/resource limits, with linked actor calls and exact revision. Fail: memorised coordinates, teacher silently completing the work, unsupported success claims, or a changed skill body reusing evidence from the prior hash.
